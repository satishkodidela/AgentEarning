# Data Products, Niche APIs, and Automated Monitoring/Alert Services as a Solo-Developer Business (2025–2026)

Research date: 2026-10-04. Method note: full-page fetches of indiehackers.com, starterstory.com, apify.com, bidassist.com, trustmrr.com and superframeworks.com were blocked by the network egress proxy in this environment. Figures attributed to those domains come from search-engine result snippets of those pages, not from reading the full page. They are probably accurate but were not checked in context; treat them as "snippet-level" evidence. Dates are given where the source shows them. Undated items are marked.

## 1. Which solo/indie data businesses have revenue (marketplaces, API sellers, lead/intent signals, government-data repackaging)?

### Takeaway
There is solid revenue evidence for solo or tiny-team **scraping/data APIs** (about $10k–$22k/month) and for the **Apify Store** as a distribution channel (top creators earn more than $10k/month, but the average is about $470/month). The evidence for "list a dataset on Datarade or AWS Data Exchange and earn passively" is weak. Lead and intent-signal products (new stores, new companies, job posts) are proven categories. They are run by small bootstrapped teams, and India has an active paid market for new-company (MCA) lead lists.

### Cited Findings
**Scraping/data APIs (solo or tiny team)**
- Scrapingdog, a web-scraping API built from India by Manthan Koolwal, earns about $22,000/month (Starter Story headline says "$264K/year"; interview undated, likely 2023–2024) — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/how-i-bootstrapped-a-264k-year-data-scrapping-api-from-india) (snippet-level)
- MrScraper: Cahyo Subroto bought an existing data-scraping business and grew it to about $1.7M/year (~$150k+/month) by automating data extraction for companies (undated) — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/stories/mrscraper) (snippet-level)
- An Indie Hackers founder grew a scraping API (social-media angle) to $10k+ MRR in 12 months through niche targeting, efficient infrastructure and steady marketing. The summary notes that social-media scraping is "high demand with high fragility": platforms keep breaking third-party access, so reliability and freshness are the real product (post undated) — [Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/growing-a-scraping-api-to-10k-mrr-in-12-months-6iF8SJRF4WpciDff9aYi) (snippet-level)

- Zuplo's API-monetization playbook projects a solo API timeline of $1K MRR in months 3–4 and $10K MRR in months 6–12. This is a vendor's **projection, not data** — [Zuplo](https://zuplo.com/learning-center/from-zero-to-1m-mrr-api-monetization-indie-hackers)

For pay-per-result and pay-per-event actors, Apify first deducts platform run costs and then takes 20% of the profit — [Apify Help](https://help.apify.com/en/articles/8684010-make-money-publishing-your-actors-on-apify-store); [use-apify.com](https://use-apify.com/docs/apify-for-developers/monetize-actors)

### Inferences
- The proven solo data-business shapes in 2025–2026 are (a) a self-serve API or actor with usage pricing, sold through SEO and marketplaces, and (b) a recurring "fresh list" email/CSV feed such as new companies or new stores. One-off dataset listings on Datarade, ADX or Snowflake show little evidence of producing solo income.
- Apify is the lowest-friction way to test demand: there is no billing or infrastructure to build, and the store already has buyer traffic. Monetizing a $2–5k/month business there would require top-decile actor performance (the average is about $470/month). Apify also has many near-identical actors in tender, SAM.gov and Shopify niches (see Q3), so commodity scrapers quickly face price pressure.
- RapidAPI's 25% fee from Nov 2025, plus uncertainty under Nokia ownership, makes it a weaker home than running your own Stripe billing or Apify (inference).
- India's MCA new-company lead market shows buyers pay ₹6k–₹60k/month for daily fresh lists. The incumbents mostly sell director phone numbers and emails, which carries personal-data risk (see Q4). A solo entrant could sell a **company-level** feed (CIN, activity code, capital, address, ROC) enriched with signals such as GST registration and website/domain found. That would be lower risk but also lower value.

### Gaps
- No verified revenue data was found for a solo seller on Datarade, Snowflake Marketplace or AWS Data Exchange.
- No 2025–2026 Acquire.com listing of a data/API business with TTM revenue and multiple was found. The search returned only Acquire-scraper actors.
- The Scrapingdog and MrScraper interview dates could not be confirmed because Starter Story fetches were blocked.
- Revenue figures for Store Leads, the Indian MCA list vendors, and Apify's individual top actors were not found.

## 2. Monitoring/alert products: which have indie revenue evidence, and which niches are under-served?

### Takeaway
General-purpose website-change monitoring is a crowded, cheap category ($9–$14/month entry tiers), and even the category leader Visualping is VC-funded with modest estimated revenue. Indie economics look better in **vertical alert products with a "pricing umbrella"** from expensive incumbents: competitor price tracking ($99–$799/month), grant discovery ($179–$499/month), and tender/RFP alerts (€49–150/month in the EU, $10k+/year GovWin in the US). Consumer urgency niches such as visa-slot alerts show strong demand but have free Telegram competition and ToS risk. No 2025–2026 verified MRR figure was found for an indie monitoring product.

Latka estimates 2023 revenue at $678.9K with 32 employees; that figure looks low for the headcount and Latka data is unreliable — [GeekWire 2021](https://www.geekwire.com/2021/visualping-raises-2m-help-users-track-website-changes-find-open-covid-19-vaccinations/); [Vancouver Tech Journal](https://vantechjournal.substack.com/p/visualping-raises-6m-to-build-ai); [Latka](https://getlatka.com/companies/visualping)

**Social/SERP signal costs (affects SERP/social monitoring products)**
- On February 6, 2026, X moved new developers to pay-per-use pricing: $0.005 per post read and $0.01 per post created, capped at 2M reads/month, with no free tier. Legacy Basic ($200/month) and Pro ($5,000/month) remain only for existing subscribers, and Enterprise starts around $42k/month — [Postproxy 2026](https://www.postproxy.dev/blog/x-api-pricing-2026/); [Roboin, 2026-02-08](https://roboin.io/article/en/2026/02/08/x-transitions-api-to-pay-per-use-model-ending-free-plan/); [Zernio](https://zernio.com/blog/twitter-api-pricing)

### Inferences
- Horizontal change monitoring is commoditized: Distill offers 25 free monitors, ChangeTower starts at $12/month, and funded players and SEO-driven newcomers like PageCrawl are already writing vertical landing pages. A solo developer should not build another generic monitor.
- Indie-friendly shapes are **vertical alert feeds** where the job is "find the needle and explain it". Examples are tenders, grants, recalls and regulatory filings in a specific niche, priced under an expensive incumbent. Instrumentl at $179–$499/month and GovWin at $10k+/year leave room for $19–$79/month niche alternatives.
- **SERP/rank-change products now carry extra legal and supply risk.** Google's DMCA §1201 suit against SerpApi means building on scraped Google SERPs, directly or through SerpApi, has new platform risk. **X/Twitter monitoring** is now cost-predictable at $0.005/read, which favours narrow keyword-alert products over bulk-ingestion ones.
- Visa-slot alerts have proven, intense demand in India but a weak business case. Free Telegram groups compete, slot-checking needs logged-in access to the scheduling portal (a ToS and account-ban risk), and auto-booking features are a clear abuse vector. Alert-only, user-run browser extensions shift the scraping to the user, which may be why most offerings are extensions.

## 3. Government procurement/tender alert services (India GeM/CPPP, EU TED, US SAM.gov): competitors, pricing, gaps

### Takeaway
All three regions have plentiful official data, and raw tender feeds are already commoditized. Apify actors sell TED, SAM.gov and GeM/CPPP records for about $0.001–$0.05 per record. Price ranges differ sharply:
- **India**: aggregators are very cheap (₹2,500–7,000/year) and paid add-ons are charged per document.
- **EU**: alert platforms run about €49–150/month.
- **US**: anywhere from about $99/month to $40k+/year (GovWin).

The gaps a solo developer could fill are interpretation rather than raw data: vertical focus, eligibility and requirements extraction, award/price intelligence, and state/local coverage.

### Cited Findings
**India**
- BidAssist offers free SMS/email tender alerts. Paid plans cost $14.50/month (billed quarterly) or $24.50/month for all regions, "from as low as Rs 3000/year". After the trial, searching is free but downloading a tender's documents costs Rs 299 per tender — [BidAssist pricing](https://bidassist.com/pricing) (snippet-level); [BidAssist home](https://bidassist.com/)
- TenderSuggest: Basic ₹2,500/year, Standard ₹7,000/year, or pay-as-you-go at ₹199 per tender — [TenderSuggest pricing](https://www.tendersuggest.com/pricing)
- TenderTiger uses quote-based subscriptions that vary by region, duration and number of user licenses — [Gartner Peer Insights](https://www.gartner.com/reviews/product/tendertiger). Tender247 aggregates central, state and organisation tenders, but no price was found — [ContraVault 2026](https://www.contravault.com/blog/7-best-tender-portals-in-india-2026)

### Inferences
- **India**: incumbents compete on breadth at very low prices, around ₹250–600/month equivalent. A solo developer cannot win on coverage. Better angles are:
  - a vertical, WhatsApp-first product that parses tender PDFs and BOQs into "Can I bid?" summaries (turnover threshold, EMD, past-experience requirements, MSE/startup exemptions);
  - tracking corrigenda and bid deadlines;
  - surfacing award/L1 price history.
  Charge ₹299–999/month per vertical, or sell to bid consultants who manage many clients. The ₹199–299 per-document fees show buyers pay for the document/analysis step, not just the alert.
- **EU**: TED is the easiest legal data source in this whole study (free, anonymous API, commercial reuse allowed). Generic TED feeds are already commoditized on Apify. The gap is a single-country, native-language, single-CPV product (for example "Polish IT tenders in English", or German construction tenders for SMEs) with AI relevance scoring, priced at €19–49/month under the €49–150 incumbents.
- **US**: the federal market is saturated (HigherGov, GovSpend, GovTribe, SamSearch, GovWin and many Apify actors). State, county, city and school-district procurement portals are fragmented and harder to scrape, so coverage there is thinner. A niche such as K-12 ed-tech RFPs or municipal IT RFPs in a few states is a plausible solo product, but it needs maintenance per portal.
- Across all regions, delivery that works is email digests plus Slack/WhatsApp/Telegram for urgent matches, with an optional CSV/API tier for consultants.

## 4. Legal and ethical boundaries of scraping in 2025–2026, and which sources are safe

### Takeaway
US case law still broadly protects **logged-out scraping of public pages**: hiQ's Ninth Circuit CFAA ruling, Meta v. Bright Data (2024), and the dismissal of X Corp v. Bright Data (2024). But the risk has shifted to other legal theories:
- **contract/ToS claims for logged-in access** (hiQ ultimately settled and paid $500k);
- **DMCA §1201 anti-circumvention**, which Google used against SerpApi in December 2025;
- **privacy law for personal data**: GDPR (KASPR fined by CNIL in 2025) and India's DPDP Act.

Infrastructure is also tightening, with Cloudflare blocking AI crawlers by default from July 2025. The safest sources are official open-data APIs and bulk downloads such as TED, SAM.gov and government registries, which carry no personal-contact data.

### Cited Findings
- **hiQ v. LinkedIn (final, 2022, older)**: in April 2022 the Ninth Circuit held that scraping publicly accessible data likely does not violate the CFAA. The case then ended in a December 2022 settlement: hiQ paid LinkedIn $500,000, agreed to stop scraping and delete its code and data, and stipulated that LinkedIn could establish CFAA liability (hiQ had also used fake logged-in accounts) — [ZwillGen](https://www.zwillgen.com/alternative-data/hiq-v-linkedin-wrapped-up-web-scraping-lessons-learned/); [Farella Braun + Martel](https://www.fbm.com/publications/what-recent-rulings-in-hiq-v-linkedin-and-other-cases-say-about-the-legality-of-data-scraping/)
- **Meta v. Bright Data (2024)**: on January 23, 2024 Judge Chen ruled that Meta's Facebook and Instagram terms do not bar **logged-off** scraping of public data, because a logged-off scraper is not a "user" bound by the ToS. Meta dropped the case on February 23, 2024 — [Quinn Emanuel](https://www.quinnemanuel.com/the-firm/news-events/client-alert-meta-v-bright-data-significant-decision-for-web-scraping-industry/); [Farella Braun + Martel](https://www.fbm.com/publications/major-decision-affects-law-of-scraping-and-online-data-collection-meta-platforms-v-bright-data/); [Nubela](https://nubela.co/blog/meta-lost-the-scraping-legal-battle-to-bright-data/). Lowenstein noted implications for investment advisers using scraped data — [Lowenstein](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/publications/client-alerts/meta-v-bright-data-ruling-has-important-implications-for-webscraping-activities-by-investment-advisers-im)
- **X Corp v. Bright Data**: dismissed with leave to amend on May 9, 2024 — [Proskauer New Media & Technology Law blog](https://newmedialaw.proskauer.com/tag/web-scraping/)
- **Google v. SerpApi (December 2025)**: Google alleges that SerpApi sends "hundreds of millions" of daily queries through "fake browsers" and submits fraudulent solves to SearchGuard challenges, which it says violates DMCA §1201 anti-circumvention. Google seeks damages and an injunction — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/google-sues-serpapi-466541); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/414143/google-battles-serpapi-over-scraping.html); [Above the Law, Dec 2025](https://abovethelaw.com/2025/12/google-built-its-empire-scraping-the-web-now-its-suing-to-stop-others-from-scraping-google/)
- **Reddit v. Perplexity, SerpApi, Oxylabs, AWMProxy (October 2025)**: Reddit alleges they scraped Reddit content through Google results while hiding their identities. Reddit planted a "trap" post visible only to Google's crawler, which later surfaced in Perplexity — [Search Engine Land](https://searchengineland.com/reddit-sues-perplexity-serpapi-scraping-google-463681)
- **GDPR, KASPR (CNIL)**: CNIL fined KASPR €240,000, published January 2025, for scraping LinkedIn contact details into a database of about 160M contacts. The breaches were no legal basis (Art. 6), excessive retention (Art. 5-1-e), lack of transparency (Arts. 12/14) and failure to honour access rights (Art. 15). CNIL also ordered KASPR to stop collecting data from people who had limited visibility of their contact details, with a compliance deadline of June 18, 2025 — [EDPB news 2025](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/news/2025/data-scraping-french-supervisory-authority-fined-kaspr-eu240-000_da); [ComplianceHub](https://www.compliancehub.wiki/understanding-the-french-supervisory-authoritys-eu240-000-fine-against-kaspr-for-data-scraping/). Conflict: some EDPB language versions are headlined "€200 000" — [EDPB (nl)](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/data-scraping-french-sa-fined-kaspr-eu200-000_nl). €240,000 appears in the 2025 EDPB URL and in secondary analysis.
- **India DPDP Act 2023 / DPDP Rules 2025**: Section 3(c)(ii) excludes personal data that the person has made publicly available, or that someone else must publish under Indian law. Commentators caution that the data must be genuinely public (not semi-private sources) and processed for a legitimate purpose, and they note debate over how this applies to scraping — [Tsaaro](https://tsaaro.com/blogs/regulatory-carve-outs-examining-exemptions-under-the-dpdp-act-2023-and-dpdp-rules-2025); [Law.asia](https://law.asia/india-data-scraping-regulation/); [IAPP](https://iapp.org/resources/article/operational-impacts-of-indias-dpdpa-part1)
- **Cloudflare (July 1, 2025)**: new Cloudflare domains block known AI crawlers by default, and Cloudflare launched "pay-per-crawl" so sites can charge AI bots. Cloudflare fronts roughly 20% of the web — [MIT Technology Review](https://www.technologyreview.com/2025/07/01/1119498/cloudflare-will-now-by-default-block-ai-bots-from-crawling-its-clients-websites/); [CO/AI](https://about.getcoai.com/news/cloudflare-now-blocks-ai-crawlers-by-default-and-launches-pay-per-scrape-program)
- One report says more than 26 million scrapes ignored robots.txt in March 2025. This is a secondary source citing industry data — [CO/AI](https://about.getcoai.com/news/cloudflare-now-blocks-ai-crawlers-by-default-and-launches-pay-per-scrape-program)
- **Platform APIs as the legal path**: X's pay-per-use API since February 2026 ($0.005/read) — [Postproxy](https://www.postproxy.dev/blog/x-api-pricing-2026/)
- **Official open data**: TED (free commercial reuse, anonymous API) — [TED developers' corner](https://ted.europa.eu/en/simap/developers-corner); SAM.gov (free API key) — [Cleat.ai](https://www.cleat.ai/developers/sam-gov-api)
- A 2026 vendor compliance guide covers scraping law in 7 countries (vendor content; not reviewed in full) — [cloro.dev](https://cloro.dev/blog/website-scraping-legal/)

### Inferences
Risk tiers for a solo developer:
- **Lowest risk**: official bulk downloads and APIs with open licences, such as TED, SAM.gov, data.gov.in, regulator RSS feeds, recall and patent databases, and company registries' company-level fields. Also licensed platform APIs, even paid ones like X pay-per-use.
- **Moderate risk**: logged-out scraping of public, non-personal pages (prices, product catalogs, store metadata, public job posts on ATS boards). Use polite rate limits and do not defeat CAPTCHAs or bot walls.
- **High risk**:
  - anything behind a login, which is a ToS breach (the hiQ settlement pattern);
  - anything that solves CAPTCHAs or defeats bot protection (the DMCA §1201 theory in Google v. SerpApi);
  - scraping Google SERPs or Reddit at scale;
  - selling personal contact data (names plus emails/phones), which falls under GDPR in the EU (KASPR) and is grey under India's DPDP. Director contact details in MCA filings may fall within s.3(c)(ii) because they are published under a legal obligation, but this is untested. Get legal advice before selling phone numbers.
- Visa-slot checking on usvisascheduling or VFS portals requires logged-in sessions. It falls into the high-risk ToS tier, especially with auto-booking.
- Cloudflare's default AI-bot blocking and its bot-management tools mean even "public" sites increasingly block scrapers. Products built on official data APIs avoid this cost and fragility.

### Gaps
- No 2025–2026 US appellate ruling was found that changes the hiQ / Bright Data framework. The Ryanair v. Booking.com CFAA jury verdict (2024) did not surface in searches and is not cited.
- The status or outcome of Google v. SerpApi and Reddit v. Perplexity as of October 2026 was not found.
- EU AI Act and text-and-data-mining (TDM) opt-out rules as they affect commercial scrapers were not researched.
- Specific GeM, CPPP and MCA terms of use on automated access were not reviewed.

## 5. Unit economics: infrastructure costs at small scale, and pricing models that work

### Takeaway
At solo scale, infrastructure is cheap. Plain HTTP checks on official APIs cost almost nothing, and residential proxies cost about $1–4/GB when needed. The main costs are maintenance (broken parsers) and marketplace fees (Apify 20% after compute; RapidAPI 25%). Proven pricing units are:
- per check-volume or per-page tiers (Visualping, ChangeTower);
- per-product (Prisync);
- per-app (AppFollow);
- per-1,000 records (Apify);
- per-call (X);
- flat annual pricing in India (₹2,500–7,000/year) with per-document upsells (₹199–299).

### Cited Findings
- Residential proxies in 2026 cost about $1/GB at the value end (DataImpulse $1/GB residential, $0.50/GB datacenter, $2/GB mobile, pay-as-you-go with non-expiring traffic) and $5–8/GB at the enterprise end. Decodo charges $2.75–3.75/GB on monthly plans or $4/GB pay-as-you-go — [DEV Community 2026](https://dev.to/ethan_walker995/cheapest-residential-proxy-providers-in-2026-price-per-gb-comparison-38g3); [Databay 2026](https://databay.com/blog/how-much-do-residential-proxies-cost); [DataImpulse](https://dataimpulse.com/blog/proxy-pricing-guide/); [Apify blog 2026](https://blog.apify.com/best-proxy-providers/)

### Inferences
- **Worked example (my calculation, not sourced)**: monitoring 1,000 pages hourly is about 720k fetches/month. At about 100KB/page that is about 72GB/month, roughly $72/month on $1/GB residential proxies, or near $0 if the targets are official APIs or sites without bot protection, plus a $5–20/month VPS. At $29/month per customer, about 100 customers (about $2.9k/month) would give gross margins above 90% on direct infrastructure. Parser maintenance and support time are the real cost.
- **LLM enrichment** (for example, summarizing a tender PDF into eligibility criteria) adds a per-item cost. It is affordable when you summarize only items that match a customer's filters rather than the whole corpus. This is an inference: LLM pricing was not researched here.
- **Pricing models that fit solo operation**:
  1. Flat monthly tiers by number of watched entities (keywords, CPV codes, competitors, apps).
  2. A paid "consultant/agency" tier with multiple client profiles and CSV/API export.
  3. In India, annual prepaid plans plus per-document or per-analysis micro-payments, matching the BidAssist and TenderSuggest norms.
  4. Usage-based pricing (per 1,000 records) only when selling through Apify, where billing is handled for you.
- **Path to "a few thousand USD/month"**:
  - About 100–150 customers at $19–29/month (global SME niche).
  - Or about 300–600 Indian SMEs at ₹499–999/month.
  - Or 10–20 B2B data buyers at $100–300/month (API/CSV feeds).
  - Or one top-decile Apify actor.
  The evidence above suggests the B2B feed and vertical-alert routes are more reliable than consumer alerts.
