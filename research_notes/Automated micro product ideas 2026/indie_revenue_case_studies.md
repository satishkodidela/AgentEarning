# Verified revenue evidence for solo, automation-heavy products at $2k–$10k/month (2024–2026)

*Method note for the report writer: the network proxy blocked direct page fetches for every source domain (TrustMRR, Indie Hackers, Starter Story, X, Apify blog, Acquire, RevenueCat, Substack, Medium, dev.to, Reddit), and the session's web-search budget ran out partway through. Nearly every finding below therefore comes from search-engine result summaries of the cited pages, not from reading the full pages. Figures that are the founder's own numbers on X, or that come from TrustMRR or Indie Hackers revenue pages, are the most reliable. Figures from SEO aggregator blogs (shno.co, saasranger, rockingweb, chromegoldmine, nichecheck, insightraider, cavuno and similar) are lower confidence and are labelled. Dates of X posts were worked out from the post IDs.*

## Q1. Which concrete solo products report $2k–$10k MRR in 2025–2026?

### Takeaway
Real, dated examples in or passing through the $2k–$10k/month band include a ChatGPT-folders Chrome extension (Easy Folders, ~$3.7k MRR about 6 months after launch, early 2025), a scraped AI job board (MoAIJobs, $2.3k/mo, 2025), job boards at $5k–$6.5k/mo (Work With Indies, Remote Rocketship), Apify actors (~$1k/mo per good actor; the best creators make over $10k/mo), a launch directory (Uneed, ~$7k growing to $10k/mo by July 2025), Marc Lou's DataFast analytics ($2.4k → $6.1k MRR between April and July 2025), and earlier stages of two boring SEO-driven utilities (Bank Statement Converter, ScreenshotOne). Only a few are third-party-verified; most are founder-reported.

### Inferences
- In this sample, the stage-dated, believable cases in the $2k–10k/month band are mostly: (a) marketplace-distributed tools (Chrome Web Store, Apify Store, Shopify App Store), (b) job boards and directories built on aggregated or scraped supply, and (c) boring single-purpose SEO utilities (PDF conversion, screenshot API). AI-wrapper "magic box" products show up mainly in the >$10k tier, and those founders usually have large audiences.
- Even an elite builder with a big audience (Marc Lou) has most small products below $1.2k/month. Revenue across a portfolio follows a power law, and the $2k–10k band is not the default result for a well-built small product.
- Strictly third-party-verified (Stripe-linked) 2025–2026 cases in the band are rare in what I could reach. TrustMRR is the right place to mine them, but it could not be fetched here.

### Gaps
- TrustMRR listing pages (category/country filters, for-sale listings with asking prices) could not be fetched (egress blocked), and the search budget ran out before snippet-level mining of individual listings. The catalogue therefore has few Stripe-verified 2025–2026 cases in the $2k–10k range. **This is the biggest gap. The report writer should say the verified sample is thin.**
- No 2025–2026 revenue figures were found for: WordPress plugins, Slack/Figma/Notion integrations, standalone data products/datasets, newsletters run with automation, Notion/Gumroad templates (only low-quality aggregator claims such as "top Notion template sellers make $3,200+/mo" — [insightraider](https://insightraider.com/en/answers/can-you-sell-notion-templates-on-gumroad) — not used as evidence), or alert services (Campnab and others publish none).

## Q2. What patterns repeat across the successful ones?

### Takeaway
The repeating shapes are: (1) a boring, single-job utility that matches an exact search phrase (bank statement → Excel; URL → screenshot); (2) built-in marketplace distribution (Chrome Web Store, Apify Store, Shopify App Store) instead of building an audience; (3) supply that is aggregated or scraped automatically (job boards, directories); (4) usage- or tier-based recurring pricing paid by businesses; (5) a long runway, since most verified band-reaching products took 1–3+ years. Audience-driven launches (Marc Lou) are the exception and do not transfer.

### Cited Findings
- **Boring SEO utility:** Bank Statement Converter is described as an ideal micro-SaaS because it serves a huge market (everyone has a bank account), uses simple technology, and targets one keyword ("bank statement converter pdf to excel"); ~1,000 daily visits, mostly from SEO, at ~$9.5k MRR — [Startups.email](https://startupsemail.substack.com/p/a-super-boring-saas-with-9747-in); [IH repost](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/a-super-boring-saas-with-9-747-in-monthly-recurring-revenue-313ffd4df2)
- **Pricing by usage/tiers:** BSC tiers are based on pages processed per month — [Founder Reports](https://founderreports.com/interview/bank-statement-converter/). Apify actors are billed per result or event, and the creator keeps 80% — [use-apify docs](https://use-apify.com/docs/apify-for-developers/monetize-actors); [reinventing.ai](https://www.reinventing.ai/blog/apify-actor-passive-income)
- **API products grow slowly but compound:** ScreenshotOne took 3 years to reach $10K MRR, then 8 months to reach $20K, with minimal marketing — [Startup Founder Stories](https://startupfounderstories.com/stories/dmytro-krasun-screenshotone-20k-mrr); the IH interview title is "Hitting $25k+ MRR by making his goals less ambitious" — [IH](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/tech/hitting-25k-mrr-by-making-his-goals-less-ambitious-jQRZDnzwm8DFAaS4Xrq0)
- **Marketplace distribution:** platform-specific micro-SaaS (Shopify, Slack, Salesforce) is recommended because "these platforms provide the audience" — [summary of rethinklab playbook](https://rethinklab.co/blog/from-0-to-10k-mrr-a-2026-indie-hacker-playbook) (opinion). Real case: a solo Shopify app reached $6.5k MRR and sold for $250k — [IH](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/getting-out-of-the-freelancing-game-by-building-a-100k-mrr-shopify-app-portfolio-qdReVAgLjz6EpW4OrJSI?commentId=P9FWwoV9CE6MUJQFWMK7)
- **Chrome extension economics depend on user count:** typical freemium conversion is 0.5–2%, high-intent niches (developer tools, SEO) reach 3–5%, and AI extensions 3–6%. Benchmarks: 10k–50k users ≈ $500–$2,500/mo; 50k–200k users ≈ $2,500–$10,000/mo — [Chromegoldmine](https://chromegoldmine.com/blog/chrome-extension-monetization/chrome-extension-revenue-benchmarks/) (AGG, vendor content). At $10–25/mo pricing, 200–500 paying users gives $3k–$10k MRR — [search summary of ExtensionPay/Chromegoldmine](https://chromegoldmine.com/blog/profitable-chrome-extension-niches/)
- **Job boards built on automated or aggregated supply:** MoAIJobs started with aggregated AI job listings and makes $2.3K/mo at 99% margin; Remote Rocketship scrapes company websites daily — [Gaps](https://gaps.com/job-boards/); [Cavuno](https://cavuno.com/blog/job-board-monetization)
- **Directories sell visibility to makers:** Uneed's revenue mixes paid launches, submissions, premium spots, ads, newsletter sponsorships and reviews — [search summary of Starter Story / Sanlis](https://www.starterstory.com/uneed-breakdown)
- **TrustMRR growth by category, H1 2026:** the median startup grew only 1.6% month-over-month from Jan to Jun 2026, trending down since March. Growing: Entertainment & Media +72% (n=17), Education +15% (n=69), Health & Fitness +10% (n=36). Declining: Gaming −16% (n=15), Creator Economy −13% — [Marc Lou X, 2026-07-27](https://x.com/marclou/status/2081666022223020540)
- **MicroConf survey patterns:** 39% of independent SaaS founders are solo (2024 report); teams of 3 founders grow 2–3x faster; 30% fewer hires in 2023; 6% fewer founders seeking funding — [MicroConf X, 2024-07-11](https://x.com/MicroConf/status/1811478998217634242); [Startups for the Rest of Us ep. 721](https://www.startupsfortherestofus.com/episodes/episode-721-7-key-takeaways-from-the-2024-state-of-independent-saas-report). Reported via secondary summary: 50% rely on communities and referrals; 70% ask for a credit card up front for trials — [Freemius 2025 State of Micro-SaaS](https://freemius.com/blog/state-of-micro-saas-2025/) (not verified against the primary source)
- **Audience is a confounder:** the founder of Easy Folders, Uneed and similar products built in public; DataFast's fast climb rode Marc Lou's audience — [X](https://x.com/marclou/status/1950900252481130634)

### Inferences
- The most repeatable low-effort shape in this evidence is **a narrow utility with an exact-match search keyword or marketplace listing, priced per usage tier and bought by small businesses or professionals** (BSC, ScreenshotOne, Apify actors). It takes years to compound, but it needs little ongoing selling.
- **Products that sell to indie hackers** (directories, launch platforms, analytics for indie founders, boilerplates) can reach the band, but they depend on the founder's presence in that community and compete in a crowded, low-budget market. TrustMRR's "Creator Economy −13%" trend hints at saturation (inference).
- Freemium Chrome extensions need roughly 50k+ users to reach the band, unless the extension is a paid power tool for a high-intent niche such as the ChatGPT power users of Easy Folders.

### Gaps
- No primary MicroConf 2025/2026 report numbers were reachable. The Freemius 2025 micro-SaaS report could not be fetched.
- No systematic data was found on the share of B2B vs B2C among products in the band.

## Q3. Typical time-to-first-revenue and time-to-$3k MRR

### Takeaway
On TrustMRR's verified data the average time to first $1 is ~5 months. Among individual cases, the time to ~$3k MRR ranges from ~3–6 months (audience-backed or hot-trend products: Easy Folders, DataFast, Lorik Morina) to 1–3 years for boring SEO/API utilities (Bank Statement Converter in year 2; ScreenshotOne needed ~3 years to reach $10k). Planning for 12–24 months is realistic; under 6 months is the exception.

- Job-board timeline from a vendor (low confidence): months 1–3 $0–500/mo; 4–6 $500–2k; 7–12 $2k–5k; year 2+ $5k–20k+ — [Cavuno](https://cavuno.com/blog/job-board-ideas)
- "Average time to $10K MRR is 21.4 months; AI/ML founders reach it 3.5x faster" — appears in aggregator summaries ([rethinklab](https://rethinklab.co/blog/from-0-to-10k-mrr-a-2026-indie-hacker-playbook)) with **no traceable primary dataset**. Treat it as unverified.
- Mobile comparison: 17.3% of subscription apps reach $1,000/month within two years, ~5% reach $10,000, and the median app earns ~$72/month one year after launch — [RevenueCat State of Subscription Apps 2025](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025) (via search summary; exact edition/year of each stat not confirmed; see also [TechCrunch 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/12/most-subscription-mobile-apps-dont-make-money-new-report-shows/))

### Inferences
- A reasonable planning range for a solo, low-audience automated product: first revenue in 1–6 months; $3k MRR in **9–24 months** if it finds fit at all. Getting there in under 6 months needs a hot trend (ChatGPT add-ons), a big existing audience, or paid ads that already work.
- SEO-driven utilities have a slow J-curve and then compound (BSC went from ~$4k/mo average in 2022 to ~$42k/mo in Aug 2025).

### Gaps
- No dataset gives a median time-to-$3k MRR specifically. TrustMRR exposes enough history to compute one, but it was not accessible.

## Q4. What fraction of launched indie products reach this level?

### Takeaway
On TrustMRR, the best verified dataset, the median startup makes **$334/month**, only 44% have *ever* made $1,000 in total, and lifetime revenue of $1k–$10k covers just 18%. Products at a sustained $2k–$10k MRR are probably in the top ~10–20% of products that bother to connect Stripe, and a smaller share of everything launched. Mobile data (RevenueCat) agrees: ~17% reach $1k/month within two years and ~5% reach $10k.

### Cited Findings
- TrustMRR (as of Nov 2025, ~840–990 verified startups): 81% made $1+; 44% made $1,000+ (lifetime); 4% made $1M+; **median total revenue per startup $1,618; median monthly revenue $334** — [Marc Lou X, 2025-11-18](https://x.com/marc_louvion/status/1990825837927805155)
- Lifetime revenue distribution on TrustMRR: 19.8% made $0; 34.3% made $1–$1k; 18% made $1k–$10k; 15.1% made $10k–$100k; 9.1% made $100k–$1M; 3.1% made $1M–$10M; 0.4% made $10M+ — [Marc Lou X, 2025-11-19](https://x.com/marclou/status/1991233562612498669)
- Independent analysis of 986 TrustMRR solo startups: only 528 had positive revenue; startups listed for sale had a **median MRR of $727**; the IQR outlier threshold was ~$16,400 MRR (103 outliers); ~38% of the core set was listed for sale. The author warns that the database over-represents very early, stalled and community-joining projects — [One Million Goal](https://www.onemilliongoal.com/p/what-986-solo-startups-actually-look)
- TrustMRR scale: 840+ verified startups, >$1B total verified revenue, 82 countries — [Product Hunt / TrustMRR](https://www.producthunt.com/products/trustmrr); [TrustMRR about](https://trustmrr.com/about). It reports 171 acquisitions in 365 days — [TrustMRR](https://trustmrr.com/why-sell-on-trustmrr). Later 2026 third-party data products cite 8,000+ startups — [BigIdeasDB](https://bigideasdb.com/trustmrr)
- Secondary claims attributed to MicroConf survey data (~700 founders): ~70% under $1,000 MRR; 18% at $1K–$5K MRR; median *profitable* micro-SaaS ~$4.2K MRR; top 1% over $50K MRR; founders at $5K–$10K MRR up 30%+ year over year in 2025 — [shno.co](https://www.shno.co/marketing-statistics/micro-saas-launch-statistics); [saasranger](https://saasranger.com/blog/micro-saas-revenue-reality-what-1000-founders-actually-earn/); [rockingweb](https://www.rockingweb.com.au/micro-saas-revenue-analysis-2025/). **Aggregator figures, not traced to the primary MicroConf report. Low confidence.** The claim that "95% reach profitability in the first year" in the same summaries is implausible and should not be used.
- MicroConf conference attendees are skewed: 28% of 230 founders reported >$100K MRR — the extreme right tail — [search summary of MicroConf founder pages](https://microconf.com/founders/0-10k-arr)
- Apify: average ~$470/developer/month across ~3,000 developers; "plenty of published actors earn nothing" — [reinventing.ai](https://www.reinventing.ai/blog/apify-actor-passive-income)
- RevenueCat (mobile subscriptions): ~17.3% reach $1k/month within 2 years, ~5% reach $10k/month, median ~$72/month at one year — [RevenueCat](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps-2025)
- What a band-level product is worth on exit: Acquire.com's 2025 report found a 3.7x profit multiple for businesses under $100k net income (3.9x for $100k–$1M), 136 reported deals, median 90 days on market; private-market SaaS revenue multiples run ~1–3x — [Acquire.com multiples report 2025 (PDF)](https://blog.acquire.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Acquire-Multiples-Report-2025.pdf); [Acquire blog Jan 2026](https://blog.acquire.com/acquire-com-biannual-acquisition-multiples-report-jan-2026/); [Acquire SaaS multiples](https://blog.acquire.com/saas-valuation-multiples/)

### Inferences
- **Survivorship and selection bias:** TrustMRR includes failures (19.8% at $0) but only among founders motivated enough to connect Stripe. Abandoned projects that never connect payments are missing, so the true share of launched products reaching $2k+ MRR is lower than any figure here. Case-study sites (Starter Story, MRR Story, IH) are selected for success by design.
- Rough synthesis (inference, not a measured statistic): ~10–20% of products tracked on revenue dashboards ever sustain $2k–$10k MRR, and probably low single-digit percent of all launched side projects.
- On exit math, a product at $3k MRR with ~90% margin (~$32k/yr profit) would sell for roughly $100k–$120k at Acquire's ~3.7x profit multiple. The Shopify app at $6.5k MRR selling for $250k (~3.2x ARR) fits that range.

### Gaps
- Could not get TrustMRR's own `/stats` page ([link](https://trustmrr.com/stats)) or a current MRR histogram, only lifetime-revenue buckets.
- No primary-source MicroConf 2025/2026 distribution, and no Stripe or Indie Hackers platform-wide distribution data was found.

## Q5. Which shapes are "mostly automated" vs. requiring constant content or sales work?

### Takeaway
The most automated band-level shapes in the evidence are self-serve SEO utilities (Bank Statement Converter), developer APIs (ScreenshotOne), and job boards/directories with scraped or aggregated supply (MoAIJobs, Remote Rocketship). Scrapers and actors look passive but need recurring maintenance when target sites change. Info products and community products need constant content or presence, and their revenue fades without it.

### Cited Findings
- **Low ops:**
  - Bank Statement Converter is run solo with zero employees — [Founder Reports](https://founderreports.com/interview/bank-statement-converter/)
  - ScreenshotOne keeps growing "despite minimal marketing efforts" — [search summary of IH/Startup Founder Stories](https://startupfounderstories.com/stories/dmytro-krasun-screenshotone-20k-mrr)
  - MoAIJobs runs at a 99% profit margin on aggregated listings — [Gaps](https://gaps.com/job-boards/)
  - Remote Rocketship checks company websites daily for openings — [Gaps](https://gaps.com/job-boards/)
  - Uneed early on ran at ~$2k/mo revenue on $66/mo costs — [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/uneed-breakdown)
- **Scrapers / Apify (looks passive, needs maintenance):** Apify's Head of Web Automation Engineering says most developers spend "5–10 hours every 3 months" maintaining scrapers. A creator with 98 actors spends 15–20 h/week (5–7 h on maintenance: selector updates, parser fixes, schema migrations). A 2026 analysis concludes it "isn't passive income — it's a real product business with a maintenance backlog, customer support, monitoring obligations" — [Apify blog](https://blog.apify.com/building-98-actors-on-apify-store/); [reinventing.ai](https://www.reinventing.ai/blog/apify-actor-passive-income); [Apify blog](https://blog.apify.com/programmer-passive-income-web-scraping/)
- **Chrome extensions:** Easy Folders reports minimal maintenance costs — [IH](https://www.indiehackers.com/product/easy-folders/6-months-post-launch-my-chrome-extension-has-hit-3-700-in-mrr-and-42-000-in-total-revenue--O3qs28VAnAkcJw0j--M). ExtensionPay (payment layer for extensions) charges 5% and claims $500k+ in public developer revenue — [search summary](https://extensionpay.com/articles/browser-extensions-make-money)
- **Content/audience-heavy (high effort, decays):** Marc Lou's course and boilerplate (CodeFast, ShipFast) fell from $39.6K/$22.3K (Apr 2025) to $20.6K/$18.2K (Jul 2025) — [X](https://x.com/marclou/status/1917560065902665964); [X](https://x.com/marclou/status/1950900252481130634) — and ShipFast was later reported at $3,537 in one month — [Streakr](https://streakr.co/playbook/marc-lou)
- **Community-dependent:** Work With Indies built a 35k-member community before monetizing — [Gaps](https://gaps.com/job-boards/)
- Automation tactics that solo micro-SaaS founders report: auto-onboarding, billing alerts, scripted updates, prewritten support replies, consolidated dashboards — [unkoa playbook](https://www.unkoa.com/micro-product-ecosystems-how-solo-founders-stack-tiny-saas-products-to-reach-5k-mrr/) (AGG/opinion)

### Inferences
Ranking by ongoing effort at $2k–$10k/month (inference from the cases above):
1. **Lowest:** self-serve SEO utility or API (file conversion, screenshot or rendering APIs) with usage-tier billing. Ongoing work is uptime and occasional support. Acquisition is slow, organic SEO.
2. **Low:** job board or directory on scraped or aggregated supply, monetized by paid listings or seeker subscriptions. Ongoing work is scraper upkeep and light curation, with some SEO.
3. **Low to medium:** Chrome extension in a high-intent niche. It is exposed to host-site DOM changes and Chrome Web Store policy.
4. **Medium:** Apify actors. Marketplace distribution is free, but target-site changes force recurring fixes. The economics favor a few well-maintained actors over large portfolios.
5. **High:** directories or launch platforms selling to indie makers, community job boards, info products, boilerplates, courses. These need constant content, presence or audience, and revenue fades when attention stops.

### Gaps
- Few founders publish hours worked per week. Except for the Apify creator, maintenance effort is inferred from qualitative statements.
- Support load (tickets/month) and churn rates for products in the band were not found.
